Background

I started inside big systems and ended up obsessed with where they fail.

Google taught me how systems scale. Startups taught me where judgment, speed, and trust break down.

Inside large systems

At Google, I worked with Tech, B2B, and Fintech companies across the US, EU, and APAC — modeling growth, running experiments, and working with C-level teams.

Inside founder reality

At Empy, I focused on conversations under pressure. At Decato.ai, I focused on creative work trapped in slow iteration.

Ex-Google. Ex-adidas. Founder of Empy.ai and Decato.ai.

I learned structure inside systems — and pressure inside startups.

Projects

Past projects taught me how work breaks. Decato is where that learning compounds.

I did not come to Decato from one clean founder arc. I came through sales, product, communication tooling, and repeated attempts to turn messy work into something usable.

Past projects

Empy.ai explored real-time intelligence for high-stakes calls. Earlier work across growth, product, and operations kept exposing the same thing: people lose time in unclear, manual, fragmented workflows.

Current project — Decato.ai

Decato turns a plan, brief, budget, and references into a furnished layout, real furniture bundle, render, and client-ready deck — compressing weeks of interior workflow into a much tighter client-feedback loop.

The throughline is simple: find expensive manual work, compress the loop, and keep the human taste where it matters.

Why Decato matters

Interior studios still redraw plans, gather references, source furniture, check fit, render, and assemble the deck by hand. Decato is trying to turn that into one coherent system.

What I Kept Noticing

The hard problems were rarely technical first.

Again and again, the failure point was not intelligence. It was broken incentives, unclear communication, and bad decisions under pressure.

The pattern

The visible problem looked technical. The real problem was almost always human coordination under pressure.

That became the thread behind what I build.

In companies

Growth breaks when teams lose clarity, attention, or alignment.

In products

A product often fails before the code does — in misunderstanding, weak loops, and quiet friction.

In founder work

The real bottleneck is often judgment under ambiguity.

What Shaped Me

Before business, games taught me how systems really work.

01 — Incentives drive behavior

People respond to rewards, friction, and status — not just good intentions.

02 — Loops are more honest than stories

The fastest way to understand a system is to watch what repeats.

03 — Skill comes from reps

You improve by iterating under feedback, not by waiting to feel ready.

Warcraft III, custom maps, and competitive play shaped how I read products, power, and behavior.

That was my first education in incentives, feedback loops, and consequences.

Founder mode started before I had permission.

Before status or polish — just the need to build something real.
The shift

The real shift was moving from career logic to builder logic.

What I learned

Building exposed reality faster than planning ever could.

What I got wrong

I became a founder when I cared more about building than being approved.
